# The Thief Who Was Beaten by a Light Bulb

**Length:** 51.6 s · **Words:** 157 · **Format:** 1080×1920, 30 fps
**Alt title:** Why Subway Bulbs Screwed In Backwards

The fact behind it: early New York subway lamps used left-hand-thread bulbs so they were
useless in a household socket. Lenny and the engineer are invented characters, in the same
way the channel uses Silas and Taro.

## Plain narration

The best thief in New York was beaten by a light bulb.
Lenny rode the brand new subway every night, and he never paid for light.
One twist, one bulb, straight under his hat.
And he wasn't alone. Half the city was doing it.
Soon, whole platforms went dark.
The subway tried guards. Lenny tipped his hat, and waited.
They tried cages. Lenny brought pliers.
Then, a quiet engineer changed one tiny thing.
That night, Lenny took a bulb home, like always.
He twisted. It wobbled. He twisted harder. It fell on his foot.
You see, every lamp in the world tightens to the right.
So the engineer cut the subway's threads backwards.
Left to tighten. Right to fall out.
It's a key cut for one single lock, and Lenny didn't own the lock.
Soon, the stealing stopped.
And Lenny? He bought his first ever ticket.
For the first time in his life, he paid for light.
