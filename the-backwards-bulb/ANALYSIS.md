# TheOddTrick — channel breakdown

Source material: the channel page (6 Shorts, 114 subscribers at time of analysis) and a
frame-by-frame + transcript study of two full videos:
*Why Coins Have Ridges on the Edge* (67.6 s) and *The Worst Carpenter in All of Japan* (59.0 s).

## 1. Strategy

| Short | Views |
|---|---|
| The Gap That Saved the Train | 60K |
| The Worst Carpenter in All of Japan | 24K |
| Why Coins Have Ridges on the Edge | 22K |
| Why Bank Vaults Have a Clock Inside | 1.5K |
| The Pirate Who Walked Under the Sea | 1.4K |
| The Astronaut Who Threw His Lunch | 1.2K |

- **Niche:** "the odd design detail that secretly solved a problem", told as a tiny fable.
- **Titles:** two templates only — `Why X Has Y` and `The [Person] Who/That [Odd Thing]`. No clickbait punctuation.
- **What wins:** the three top videos all explain an *everyday object or structure you can still see*
  (rail gaps, squeaky floors, coin ridges). The three weakest are one-off adventure stories.
  The object-you-recognise angle is the growth lever.
- **Length:** 59–68 s, vertical 1080×1920, 30 fps.

## 2. Script formula

Both analysed scripts follow the same seven beats:

| Beat | Time | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Paradox hook | 0–3 s | One sentence, starts mid-story, contains a contradiction | "The deadliest assassin in Japan was beaten by the worst carpenter in Japan." |
| 2. Named nobody | 3–8 s | A first-name-only everyman, place, and job | "Silas changed money in Old London…" |
| 3. The rule | 8–14 s | A "one X, one Y" mechanic that gets repeated | "One step, one squeak." / "One scrape was nothing, but a thousand a week filled the jar." |
| 4. Escalation | 14–30 s | Rule of three, ends on a throwaway joke | "Bread cost one coin, then two, then three." / "Even the cat was costing him money." |
| 5. The turn | ~30 s | Starts with "Then," | "Then, on a moonless night, a shadow dropped over the wall." |
| 6. "You see," explainer | ~38–50 s | Mechanism in 3 sentences, always closed with a household simile | "…like a pizza missing a slice." / "It's a rusty hinge under your feet." |
| 7. Callback ending | last 8 s | The opening rule is flipped onto the hero | "And now they paid Taro one coin for every squeak." / "Yes, the apple guy." |

Writing rules observed:
- **3.3–3.4 words per second** (196 words in 58 s; 226 in 66 s).
- Sentences average 9 words; almost none over 15. Past tense, third person, no "did you know".
- No intro, no outro, no call to action. The video ends on the punchline.
- Numbers are small and concrete (50 guards, 9,001 coins, one coin).

## 3. Visual style

- **Characters:** flat-shaded 3D "bean" people — oversized round head, pill body, hair-thin black
  limbs, thick dark outline. Faces are 2D-style: arc eyes for happy, dots for neutral, big white
  circles for shock. One signature prop per character (hat, mask, wig, apple).
- **Sets:** low-poly 3D with flat colours and simple textures (timber frames, shoji screens,
  wood grain). Warm interior palette — browns, cream, orange — with a cool blue night variant.
- **Explainer mode:** every video cuts to a **flat royal-blue void** for the "You see," section,
  with one oversized object (coin edge, nail and clamp) rotating in close-up.
- **Lighting:** one warm key light, hard shadows, soft dark vignette.

## 4. Camera

- A new shot every **2–3 seconds**, almost always on a sentence boundary.
- Every shot moves: slow push-in is the default; low angles for characters, macro for objects.
- Alternates wide establishing → close-up → object insert. Hard cuts, occasional cross-dissolve
  into the explainer.

## 5. On-screen text

- **Captions:** one word at a time, lower-middle of frame (~70% down), heavy rounded sans
  (Poppins ExtraBold look), white with a thick black outline, punctuation kept, small pop-in.
- **Keyword pops:** top of frame, same heavy font, two-line stacks in white + yellow/orange/green
  ("IRON / CLAMP", "STILL / STRAIGHT", "1 SQUEAK / = 1 COIN").
- **Sound-word bursts:** red starburst with white outline — "SQUEAK!", "CLINK!", "BANG!".

## 6. Voice and sound

- Single calm male narrator, dry and slightly amused, brisk, no vocal fry or hype.
- Light playful music bed well under the voice; a sound effect on nearly every pop and action.

## 7. How the new video applies this

*The Thief Who Was Beaten by a Light Bulb* (51.6 s) uses the same seven beats, the same
caption and pop-text system, the blue-void explainer, and a recognisable-object topic
(why some bulbs screw in backwards) — the category that performs best on the channel.
