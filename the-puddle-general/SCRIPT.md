# The Old Soldier Who Filled the Castle With Bowls of Water...

Script in the Comicron style. 229 words, about 65 seconds at a natural pace.

2. The Puddle General Who Beat 10,000 Men...
3. How a Bowl of Water Stopped an Entire Army...

## Script

An old soldier put a bowl of water in every cellar of the castle, while ten thousand men camped outside the walls. The knights were sharpening swords. He was filling bowls. The captain asked, are we fighting them or giving them a bath? The cook just wanted his soup bowls back. But he lined them up along every inch of the wall, then sat in the dark and stared at them. All night. Nobody was allowed downstairs. Not even the cat. First night, nothing. Second night, nothing. By the third night, the whole castle called him the Puddle General. And outside, the enemy wasn't even attacking. No ladders. No arrows. Just quiet. Then, on the fifth night, he jumped up and screamed for buckets. One bowl in the east cellar was shivering. Tiny rings, again and again, and nobody was touching it. You see, the enemy wasn't climbing the walls. They were digging under them. Every swing of a pickaxe sends a tiny shake through the ground. You can't feel it, but still water can. Like your coffee trembling when a truck drives past. The rings pointed straight at the tunnel. So he dug down to meet it, and the whole castle poured in their buckets. By sunrise, the tunnel was a river, and ten thousand men were soaking wet. They lost the war to a bowl of water.

## Is it true?

The trick is real history: defenders in ancient and medieval sieges detected enemy tunnels with
bowls of water, drums with dried peas, or a bronze shield that rang when diggers worked nearby.
Herodotus describes the bronze-shield version. The old soldier, the castle and the flooding are
invented for the story. This is from memory, so check it before stating it as fact in the
description.
