ABOUT THIS VIDEO
On the Bosphorus, a strait less than 800 meters wide in places, sits a city that survived more than 24 sieges and was the capital of three of history's greatest empires.

We follow Byzas and his Greek colonists from Megara to the Oracle of Delphi and her riddle, "build your city opposite the blind". Then Constantine the Great moves the capital of the Roman world east and founds New Rome. Theodosius II builds the triple walls that stopped Attila, the Avars and Persians, and the Arab Caliphates for a thousand years. The Byzantines defend their harbour with Greek Fire, a weapon whose recipe is still unknown.

Finally, 1453: the 21-year-old Sultan Mehmed II, Orban's Great Basilisk cannon, 53 days of bombardment, the iron chain across the Golden Horn, seventy warships dragged over a mountain in a single night, the last service in Hagia Sophia, the Kerkoporta gate, and the fate of Constantine XI, the last Roman Emperor.
