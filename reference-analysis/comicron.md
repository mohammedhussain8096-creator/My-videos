# Reference analysis: Comicron (@comicronn)

Channel: 56.1K subscribers, 427 videos. It started with Marvel/DC narration Shorts, then switched
to "clever trick" story Shorts in the same style as TheOddTrick. The trick stories are its biggest
hits:

| Short | Views |
|---|---|
| The Prisoner Who Watered His Wall | 3.6M |
| The Little Candle That Dethroned a King | 3.2M |
| The Guard Who Wanted To Be Locked In The Vault | 373K |
| Why Winemakers Never Fill the Bottle | 329K |
| The Thief Who Only Stole Left Shoes | 253K |
| The Bronze Whale That Emptied a City | 248K |
| Why Casinos Never Ban Their Luckiest Player | 65K |
| The King Who Built a Useless Pyramid | 64K |
| How Clay Pots Stopped an Entire Army | 20K |
| Why a Blind Man Tied Bells Deeper Into the Jungle | 12K |

The five uploaded videos were studied at 1.5 frames per second, with full transcripts, shot-cut
counts and loudness.

## 1. Titles

- Two patterns only: **"The [Person/Thing] Who/That [odd act]..."** and **"Why [thing] [odd fact]..."**.
- Always ends with **"..."**, which promises an unfinished secret.
- The two 3M+ hits are both "The [person] Who [one strange repeated act]" with a **humble person
  beating someone powerful** (prisoner vs prison, candle maker vs king).

## 2. Script formula (all five follow it)

| Beat | Time | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–6 s | One sentence: a person does something strange, often with a big number | "The king bought every clay pot in his kingdom, 300 of them, because an army of 10,000 is two days from his walls." |
| Mockery | 6–16 s | Two or three people mock it, each with a one-line joke | "Are we fighting them or pickling them?" / "Finally, enough jars for jam." |
| The secret | 16–26 s | He does one more odd step and refuses to explain | Nine holes in every lid, a covered cart, "Not even the general knows." |
| Repetition | 26–40 s | Time passes in short beats; a nickname appears | "Weeks became months. His candles grew shorter. His knees grew stiff." / "the Pot King" |
| The turn | 40–48 s | "Then," / "Suddenly," / "On the last night," | "At dawn, the ladders hit the walls." |
| The payoff | 48–58 s | The plan works in one big visual moment | "300 pots smash on 300 helmets and out pour 3 million angry bees." |
| "You see," | 45–60 s | The mechanism in 2–4 short sentences, often with an everyday comparison | "A solid wall sounds dull when you tap it. A hollow one rings." |
| Last line | final 3 s | An ironic summary or punchline, often without saying "the end" | "He never touched the king, he only measured the wax." / "the bank had locked him in with the gold and paid him for it." |

Writing rules:
- 218–253 words in 63–75 seconds (about 3.4 words per second). Longer than our 50–55 s target.
- Present tense in action scenes ("The top driver sticks his head in and goes…").
- Sentences are short; many are fragments ("Weeks became months." "The guard was gone.").
- Uses "You see," exactly as TheOddTrick does, but **later**: often only after the payoff.
- Numbers everywhere: 300 pots, 10,000 men, 40 days, 12 tons, 5 walls, 9 holes.
- Some videos use a list format ("Level 1… Level 5" in the WW2 buttons video).

## 3. Visual style

- **Characters:** pure-white round heads, heavy black outline, big expressive cartoon eyes with
  brows, sweat drops, and teeth; tall single-colour bodies (suits, robes, uniforms) with small 3D
  details (tie, badge, belt). Very long thin black legs. Close to our `makeChar3`.
- **Sets:** rich, warm, detailed 3D rooms: a throne room with red banners and candles, a bank vault
  with steel door and gold bars, a 1940s workshop full of jars and tools, a stone castle wall with
  hundreds of attackers, an icy mountain road. **Every set is crowded with props.**
- **Lighting:** strong colour themes per video: teal + orange (trucks), warm gold (workshop),
  red + blue (throne room), cool blue (vault). Soft bloom on lights and candles.
- **Explainer:** shown on objects in the scene, sometimes on a flat colour background (orange
  baffles in the truck, a blueprint for the button). Big text labels like "12 TONS", "LEVEL 3", "FRONT".
- **Effects:** glowing particles, sparks, flying bees, liquid sloshing, sweat drops, starbursts,
  ghost overlays when showing memories, lens flares on candles.

## 4. Camera and editing

- **Two modes:**
  - **Long continuous moves** (clay pots: 5 cuts; vault: 4 cuts; candle: 2 cuts in 63 s). The
    camera glides through the set, around characters, into objects.
  - **Fast cutting** for action and lists (fuel truck: 23 cuts; buttons: 20 cuts).
- Extreme close-ups on faces for every joke line, then pull back to reveal the set.
- Slow push-ins on objects that matter (the candle, the vault door, the button).
- Captions: one or two words, white with black outline, middle of the frame. Small headers like
  "LONDON · 1940".

## 5. Sound

- Natural voice, steady level (−14 to −18 dB average), music very low.
- Effects only on actions: clinks, smashes, buzzing, engine rumble.

## 6. What to take for our channel

1. Title: "The [humble person] Who [one strange act]..." ending in "...".
2. Hook with a big number in the first sentence.
3. Two mocking one-liners as jokes; a nickname for the hero.
4. A secret step the hero won't explain.
5. One huge, visual payoff moment (bees, an empty vault, a crown lifted off).
6. "You see," with an everyday comparison, then a short ironic last line.
7. 63–67 seconds; warm, prop-heavy 3D sets; long gliding camera moves; close-ups on jokes.
