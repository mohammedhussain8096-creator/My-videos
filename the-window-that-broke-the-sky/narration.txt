The fastest plane in the world was beaten by its own windows.
In nineteen fifty two, Britain built the Comet, the first jet airliner, faster than anything in the sky.
Passengers loved its big, square windows. It was like flying in a living room.
Then, one Comet broke apart over the sea. Then another.
Investigators checked the engines. Fine. The fuel. Fine.
So they sank a whole plane in a giant water tank, and pumped it up and down, flight after pretend flight.
Three thousand flights later, the metal split, right at the corner of a window.
You see, every flight, the cabin puffs up like a balloon, then shrinks.
That stress flows through the metal like water. A curve lets it slide around.
But a sharp corner traps it. It piles up, until the metal tears.
It's like ripping paper. Start at a notch, and it goes in a second.
So engineers rounded every corner. Next time you fly, look. Not a single sharp corner.
The square windows were the most beautiful thing on that plane. And the most dangerous.
