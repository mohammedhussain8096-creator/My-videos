The worst plumber in London bent every pipe he touched, and it saved the city's noses.
Albie fixed sinks in old London, where every pipe ran straight down to the sewer.
Straight was cheap. Straight was fast. And straight let the sewer breathe right back up.
Every kitchen smelled like the river. People blamed the fish. Then the cat.
But Albie put a wiggle in every pipe, like a sleeping snake.
His boss went red as a brick. A bent pipe? Did you sit on it?
So he fired Albie, and hung up a new sign. Straight pipes only.
Then came a summer so hot, the river began to cook.
The whole city reeked. Even the boss wore a clothes peg on his nose.
Except one street. Albie's street smelled of fresh bread.
You see, a straight pipe is an open door.
But a bend always keeps a little water behind.
And smell can't swim. It's a cork made of water.
There's one under your sink right now.
By autumn, the boss had a new sign. Bent pipes only.
